\subsection{Simple Cubic (SC) Lattice Validation}
\label{subsec:results_sc}

We begin by validating our implementation of the Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) on the Simple Cubic (SC) lattice. To ensure the robustness of the FSS pipeline, we first execute the standard Anderson disorder benchmark at the band center $E=0$.

% Fit parameters: system sizes M in {4, 6, 8, 10}, polynomial order n_r = 4.

% Systematic errors are propagated by shifting y by +/- 0.08.
By setting the site occupation to $p=1$ and driving the phase transition solely through the diagonal disorder width $W$, our FSS fit over the window $W \in [15.0, 18.5]$ yields a critical point of $W_c = 16.6174 \pm 0.0156$ (stat)
\footnote{stat represents the error originating from the finite simulation data, and is calculated via the bootstrap method.}
 $\pm 0.0148$ (syst) 
\footnote{syst represents the error originating from our model assumptions. We fixed the irrelevant scaling exponent $y = 1.12$ so we estimate how much our fitted values would shift if $y$ was actually slightly different.}
 and an exponent $\nu = 1.6205 \pm 0.0085 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.0052 \text{ (syst)}$, with a correction amplitude of $b = 0.0958 \pm 0.0045 \text{ (stat)}$ and a goodness-of-fit $\chi^2/\text{dof} = 2.421$. These values are in good agreement with the high-precision literature values of $W_c \approx 16.53 \pm 0.05$ and $\nu \approx 1.571 \pm 0.007$ \cite{Slevin1999, Slevin2014}. The scale-invariant crossing of the dimensionless localization length $\Lambda_M(W)$ is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_bench_crossing}, and its associated universal FSS collapse is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_bench_collapse}.

\subsection{SC Quantum Percolation and Geometric Constraints}

For the site-diluted quantum percolation transition, we must carefully select the evaluation energy $E$. As documented in previous literature, the SC lattice is bipartite. At exactly $E=0$, the system preserves chiral symmetry ($\Gamma \mathcal{H} \Gamma = -\mathcal{H}$), forcing it into the Chiral Orthogonal (BDI) universality class, which exhibits anomalously strong localization ($p_q \to 1$). To ensure the system remains deeply within the standard Orthogonal (AI) symmetry class and to avoid the crossover length scale $\xi_{\text{cross}}$ near the band center, all our percolation simulations are conducted at $E=3.1t$.

At $E=3.1t$, we computed $\Lambda_M(p)$ for widths $M \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$. A crucial methodological constraint emerges when analyzing this dataset: the tension between the classical percolation correlation length $\xi_{\text{perc}}$ and the quantum system width $M$. The standard finite-size scaling (FSS) ansatz inherently assumes that the system is perturbatively close to the 3D quantum fixed point. However, the classical percolation threshold $p_c$ defines a non-analytic geometric singularity. For length scales $L < \xi_{\text{perc}}$, the infinite cluster is not a 3D bulk, but a fractal network with a backbone dimension of $D_B \approx 1.87$ \cite{Porto1997}.

This induces a breakdown in single-parameter scaling that can be understood strictly through the scaling theory of localization developed by Abrahams \& Anderson \cite{Abrahams1979}. The scaling beta function, $\beta(g) = d \ln g / d \ln L$, is explicitly dependent on the spatial dimension $d$. Because the fractal regime ($D_B \approx 1.87$) is strictly below the lower critical dimension for orthogonal systems ($d_{lc} = 2$), the beta function is unconditionally negative ($\beta(g) < 0$) for $L < \xi_{\text{perc}}$. A true quantum critical point can only exist in the 3D regime ($L > \xi_{\text{perc}}$).

In our Transfer Matrix simulations, the finite system width $M$ acts as the infrared cutoff for the Renormalization Group (RG) flow. For the single-parameter quantum FSS ansatz (Eq.~\ref{eq:fss_irrelevant}) to be mathematically valid, the RG integration must be overwhelmingly dominated by the 3D beta function; i.e., we require $M \gg \xi_{\text{perc}}$.

If we attempt to include the intermediate size $M=8$ in our optimization (where $\xi_{\text{perc}} \approx 4.6$ at the lower FSS window bound), the RG integration from $L=1$ to $L=8$ spends approximately $70\%$ of its ``RG time'' strictly inside the unconditionally localized fractal regime. Because the macroscopic boundary conditions of $M=8$ are dominated by this $d=1.87$ fractal RG flow, evaluating the 3D Taylor expansion of the FSS scaling function is mathematically uncontrolled. The optimizer compensates for this strongly $p$-dependent fractal suppression by driving the quantum irrelevant correction amplitude to a massive, unphysical negative value ($b \approx -1.92$), artificially biasing the critical point up to $p_q = 0.530$.

Consequently, $M=8$ is mathematically incapable of representing asymptotic 3D critical behavior. Only by restricting our analysis to the strongly Euclidean regime ($M \ge 10$) can we rigorously isolate the macroscopic quantum transport threshold without topological contamination.

Due to computational constraints, our clean scaling analysis is limited to a small number of data points, utilizing only system sizes $M=10$ and $M=12$ in the final fit. Because we only have two system sizes, the fit is underconstrained; we must fix the irrelevant exponent to $y = 1.12$, and the optimizer is highly sensitive to the initial parameters. We obtained our results only by utilizing a high-precision initial guess ($p_q \approx 0.502$) derived from the linear crossings of the curves, without which the solver gets trapped in unphysical local minima. Within the window $p \in [0.46, 0.54]$, we extract a critical threshold of $p_q = 0.51670 \pm 0.00456 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.00450 \text{ (syst)}$ and an exponent of $\nu = 1.5992 \pm 0.1107 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.1119 \text{ (syst)}$. Despite the underconstrained nature of the fit, the quality is high, yielding a robust $\chi^2/\text{dof} = 1.068$ and a goodness-of-fit probability $Q = 0.362$. Furthermore, the critical exponent $\nu$ is consistent with the 3D Orthogonal universality class ($\nu \approx 1.622$) within statistical error. However, our current data is insufficient to fully characterize the transition or resolve the systematic shift in $p_q$ relative to the asymptotic literature value ($p_q \approx 0.50628$) \cite{Ujfalusi2014}. Additional data, particularly at larger system sizes ($M \ge 14$), is necessary to perform a fully unconstrained scaling analysis. The dimensionless localization length curves are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_dilut_crossing}, and their universal scaling collapse is presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_dilut_collapse}.
