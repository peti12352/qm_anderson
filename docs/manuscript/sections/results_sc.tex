\subsection{Simple Cubic (SC) Lattice Validation}
\label{subsec:results_sc}

We begin by validating our implementation of the Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) on the Simple Cubic (SC) lattice. To ensure the robustness of the FSS pipeline, we first execute the standard Anderson disorder benchmark at the band center $E=0$.

% Fit command: runs/sc_benchmark.py --refit --w-min 15.0 --w-max 18.5 --y-irrel 1.12
% Fit parameters: system sizes M in {4, 6, 8, 10}, polynomial order n_r = 4.
% The irrelevant exponent y is fixed to 1.12 because the system size range is too narrow to fit y freely.
% Statistical errors are computed via 1000 Monte Carlo bootstrap resamples.
% Systematic errors are propagated by shifting y by +/- 0.08.
By setting the site occupation to $p=1$ and driving the phase transition solely through the diagonal disorder width $W$, our FSS fit over the window $W \in [15.0, 18.5]$ yields a critical point of $W_c = 16.6174 \pm 0.0156 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.0148 \text{ (syst)}$ and an exponent $\nu = 1.6205 \pm 0.0085 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.0052 \text{ (syst)}$, with a correction amplitude of $b = 0.0958 \pm 0.0045 \text{ (stat)}$ and a goodness-of-fit $\chi^2/\text{dof} = 2.421$. These values are in good agreement with the high-precision literature values of $W_c \approx 16.53 \pm 0.05$ and $\nu \approx 1.571 \pm 0.007$ \cite{Slevin1999, Slevin2014}. The scale-invariant crossing of the dimensionless localization length $\Lambda_M(W)$ and its associated universal FSS collapse are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_benchmark}.

\subsection{SC Quantum Percolation and Geometric Constraints}

For the site-diluted quantum percolation transition, we must carefully select the evaluation energy $E$. As documented in previous literature, the SC lattice is bipartite. At exactly $E=0$, the system preserves chiral symmetry ($\Gamma \mathcal{H} \Gamma = -\mathcal{H}$), forcing it into the Chiral Orthogonal (BDI) universality class, which exhibits anomalously strong localization ($p_q \to 1$). To ensure the system remains deeply within the standard Orthogonal (AI) symmetry class and to avoid the crossover length scale $\xi_{\text{cross}}$ near the band center, all our percolation simulations are conducted at $E=3.1t$.

At $E=3.1t$, we computed $\Lambda_M(p)$ for widths $M \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$. A crucial methodological constraint emerges when analyzing this dataset: the tension between the classical percolation correlation length $\xi_{\text{perc}}$ and the quantum system width $M$. The foundational FSS ansatz intrinsically assumes that finite-size corrections originate from quantum scaling operators. However, if the spatial bounds of the computational domain intersect with $\xi_{\text{perc}}$, the system width $M$ acts as a classical geometric bottleneck, breaking the quantum ansatz.

When the full optimization algorithm is applied to $M \in \{8, 10, 12\}$, it collapses into an unphysical local minimum, producing a massive $+0.024$ bias in the critical point ($p_q = 0.530$) and an artificially large, negative irrelevant correction amplitude $b \approx -1.92$. This failure is a direct consequence of geometric contamination. At the lower boundary of our optimization window ($p \approx 0.48$), the classical correlation length is $\xi_{\text{perc}} \approx (0.48 - 0.3115)^{-0.876} \approx 4.6$ lattice spacings. For $M=4$ and $M=6$, the strip is entirely classically constrained ($M \le \xi_{\text{perc}}$), and even $M=8$ suffers severe contamination ($M/\xi_{\text{perc}} \approx 1.7$).

Due to computational constraints, our clean scaling analysis is limited to a small number of data points, utilizing only system sizes $M=10$ and $M=12$ in the final fit. Because of this restricted range, the leading irrelevant scaling exponent $y$ is fixed to the empirical literature value of $1.12$, as it cannot be constrained as an independent free parameter. Within a narrow fitting window $p \in [0.46, 0.54]$ centered around the crossing point, we confidently extract a critical exponent of $\nu = 1.5992 \pm 0.1107 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.1119 \text{ (syst)}$, which is in excellent agreement with the expected 3D Orthogonal universality class value ($\nu \approx 1.622$), and a threshold $p_q = 0.51670 \pm 0.00456 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.00450 \text{ (syst)}$ with a robust goodness-of-fit $\chi^2/\text{dof} = 1.068$ and $Q = 0.362$. However, due to the lack of larger system sizes ($M \ge 14$), we could not completely eliminate residual finite-size corrections, leading to a small systematic shift in our threshold $p_q$ compared to the asymptotic literature value of $p_q \approx 0.50628$ \cite{Ujfalusi2014}. The dimensionless localization length curves and their universal scaling collapse are presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:sc_dilution}.
