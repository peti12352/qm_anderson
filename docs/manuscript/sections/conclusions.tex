\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In summary, we have investigated the metal-insulator transition in the three-dimensional Simple Cubic (SC) lattice using a JAX-accelerated implementation of the Transfer Matrix Method (TMM). We studied both the standard Anderson model with continuous on-site potential disorder and the site-diluted quantum percolation model.

For the Anderson model at the band center $E=0$, our solver successfully reproduced the known critical point and exponent, yielding $W_c = 16.6174 \pm 0.0156 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.0148 \text{ (syst)}$ and $\nu = 1.6205 \pm 0.0085 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.0052 \text{ (syst)}$, validating the correctness and numerical stability of our implementation. 

For the site-diluted quantum percolation transition at $E=3.1t$, we analyzed the dimensionless localization length curves. We identified a classical percolation crossover that contaminates small system widths ($M \le 8$), causing severe finite-size distortions. By restricting our scaling analysis to uncontaminated system sizes ($M \ge 10$), and despite the limitation of having only two system sizes ($M=10, 12$) available for the final fit, we resolved the quantum percolation threshold $p_q = 0.51670 \pm 0.00456 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.00450 \text{ (syst)}$ with a critical exponent $\nu = 1.5992 \pm 0.1107 \text{ (stat)} \pm 0.1119 \text{ (syst)}$ in excellent agreement with the expected 3D Orthogonal universality class ($\nu \approx 1.622$). 

Our work highlights the importance of accounting for classical geometric features when studying quantum phase transitions on diluted lattices. Future studies can extend these high-precision calculations to larger system sizes and other 3D geometries.
